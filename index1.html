System Scope & Technical Specifications

Detailed parameters matching the problem statement architecture.

1. Deployment Environment

Single residential household setup, replicated in a university laboratory for controlled testing and evaluation.

2. Electrical Connection

Single-phase 230V AC connection (50Hz), standard for Indian residential households covering bulk household electrical loads.

3. Hardware & Sensors

Current sensors (ACS712 / SCT-013) paired with ESP32 Wi-Fi IoT smart modules capturing voltage, current, active power, and energy.

4. Appliances Considered

Representative set of 7 appliances: Refrigerator, Air Conditioner, Washing Machine, Geyser, Lighting Circuit, Television, and Plug Loads.

5. Sampling & Aggregation

Data sampled every 1 second for real-time anomaly detection, aggregated to 1-minute intervals for model training and forecasting.

6. NILM Ground Truth

Appliance on/off state labels recorded separately to serve as ground truth for training and validating disaggregation models.

Problem Definition & Context

Worldwide electricity demand is rising fast enough to put real strain on generation and distribution infrastructure. According to the IEA's Electricity report, global consumption grew by 4.3% in recent years, driven by electrification, data centers, and developing economies. Meanwhile, residential and small-commercial consumers remain stuck with conventional electromechanical or basic digital meters read by hand.

Core Problems Addressed:

  • Lack of Real-Time Visibility: Consumers only find out about high usage once the bill arrives, by which point faulty appliances have been wasting power for weeks.
  • No Appliance-Level Insight: Aggregate meters can't pinpoint specific culprits—old refrigerator, failing geyser, or badly tuned HVAC.
  • Undetected Anomalies & Faults: Sudden jumps caused by equipment malfunction, wiring faults, or unauthorized loads go unnoticed.
  • Inefficient Demand-Side Management: Utilities lack granular edge data to forecast peak load or run effective demand-response programs.
  • Rising Cost Pressure: Consumers with no way to monitor or adjust usage end up paying significantly more.
